# Eurodrone schedule slips again; first flight pushed to 2028-2029, entry into service beyond 2030
Date: 2026-07-02
Source: Meta-Defense / Xinhua / Airbus
URL: https://meta-defense.fr/en/2026/07/02/eurodrone-delay-airbus-dassault-tensions/
---
The German Ministry of Defense reported in May 2024 that Eurodrone's delayed schedule had already produced additional costs of approximately €1.4 billion, attributed to coordination problems between prime contractor Airbus and subcontractor Dassault (source: https://english.news.cn/europe/20240517/21336a3a790d4e9f8ef267865af2bc3d/c.html). The original 2016 plan targeted initial capability in 2025; OCCAR acknowledged a further one-year slip in autumn 2025, and by mid-2026 reporting placed first flight around 2028 with entry into service not envisaged before 2031 (source: https://meta-defense.fr/en/2026/06/18/eurodrone-france-confirms-commitment-may-delay-order/). A separate June 2026 Airbus press release put first flight at 2029, versus earlier reporting of 2027 (source: https://okdiario.com/techy/en/europe-wanted-to-prove-that-it-was-capable-of-manufacturing-its-own-large-military-drone-without-relying-so-heavily-on-the-united-states-or-israel-but-the-eurodrone-has-ended-up-facing-delays-funding/5816/). In November 2025 Senate testimony, French General Jérôme Bellanger characterized the program as five years behind schedule and at risk of being obsolete on delivery (source: https://meta-defense.fr/en/2026/06/18/eurodrone-france-confirms-commitment-may-delay-order/). Construction of the first prototype had not started as of the most recent published findings (source: https://meta-defense.fr/en/2026/07/02/eurodrone-delay-airbus-dassault-tensions/).
